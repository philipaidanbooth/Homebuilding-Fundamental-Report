Driver 3 data flags (2026-11-10). Read before relying on any number.

1. SERIES MISMATCH, NOT RESOLVED. The 5.5% (building materials) and 10.3% (total inputs) figures in the NAHB article could not be reproduced from FRED because the article gives no series IDs. Closest FRED series: WPUID612 (materials and components for construction) +5.7% y/y; WPUIP2311001 (inputs to residential construction) +9.7% y/y. Only WPUIP2311101 (goods inputs, +8.7%, 164.372) ties exactly to the reference report. Pick one series and label it.
2. Verified against the earlier reports: goods-input PPI +8.7% y/y and +11.7% vs Dec 2024; No. 2 diesel PPI +115.2% y/y; median new-home price -3.9% y/y ($400,400, Sep).
3. Steel mill products +27.6% y/y and softwood lumber +14.2% y/y are consistent with a tariff story, but PPI does not isolate tariffs. Do not claim tariffs as the cause without a source that does.
4. LABOR. Construction average hourly earnings +4.7% y/y vs +3.1% for total private (Oct): modest wage pressure, not a spike. Construction employment +1.5% y/y. JOLTS construction openings +44.7% y/y is one volatile month; use a 3-month average before citing. Evidence for the immigration-enforcement hypothesis is NOT in this data: the foreign-born share of construction workers (CPS) is not on FRED and was not pulled.
5. NOT PULLED: land and lot cost per home or share of ASP (needs builder 10-Qs); PPI component detail beyond the series listed; any tariff data.
6. Lennar figures in d3_lennar_price_vs_cost.csv are carried from the earlier driver 3 draft (Lennar Q3 FY26 8-K) and were not re-verified here. Implied cost of sales is calculated as ASP x (1 - gross margin); it includes land, interest and other items, not just construction.
7. Dates differ: PPI, new-home price and JOLTS run through Sep 2026; construction earnings and employment through Oct 2026.
8. MSPNHSUS, CES0500000003 and UNRATE are reused from the Driver 1 raw folder (pulled 2026-11-09), not re-pulled. All FRED series are current vintage as of access date, not real-time (ALFRED).

9. DHI (added 2026-11-10, from 10-Qs for quarters ended Jun 30, 2026 and 2025): the text says the margin decline came from "the average cost of our homes closed increasing along with a decrease in the average selling price." My calc from the headline closing price and margin implies cost per home roughly flat to slightly DOWN (about -0.7%). I cannot reconcile this without DHI's average cost figure; quote DHI's wording and do not present the calc as DHI's cost.
10. DHI land balance per owned lot (about +16% y/y) is a rough proxy: the balance sheet figure includes land under development and consolidated Forestar, while the owned-lot count is homebuilding only. It shows direction, not a land cost per home. Treat as unverified.

11. PHM (added 2026-11-10, 10-Qs for quarters ended Sep 30, 2026 and 2025): cost per closing is calculated from reported home sale cost of revenues / closings. It is all-in (land, construction, capitalized interest amortization), not a pure land or construction cost, and mix (PHM's ASP is about $561K vs Lennar's $372K and DHI's $362K) differs a lot across builders. PHM cost per home ROSE while Lennar's fell; do not average across builders.
12. PHM quotes (MD&A, Q3 FY26): "we continue to face pressure in the cost of land acquisition and development. Due to the length of our land development and construction cycle times, there is a lag between when such cost changes occur and when they impact our operating results." The Q3 FY25 10-Q says the same and attributes margin declines to "higher land costs combined with ... elevated sales incentives." No tariff or immigration language found in the PHM filings.
